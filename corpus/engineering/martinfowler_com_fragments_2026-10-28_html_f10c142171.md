# [martin-fowler] Fragments: October 28

> Source: engineering | Category: engineering | 2026-10-28T13:50:35Z

Fragments: October 28

Andrej Karpathy sees  a widening gap  in how people see LLM capabilities. 

 
   The general public has mostly not interacted with these systems. When they have, it looks like a derpy chatbot. The majority of professionals still see only a modest uplift. And a small sliver of professionals are experiencing the vertigo of the curve going vertical. And it is all happening at the same time. 
 

 Many of my colleagues are living in a world where LLM capabilities are stunning, but it’s easy to forget that’s not what most people experience. One thing I do is use free-tier LLM chatbots, and I’m still finding that while most of the time they are useful, their unreliability undermines much of that usefulness. Yes, you can argue that I’m not using frontier tools or a forceful enough harness, but that’s true of most regular folks too. To understand their perception, it’s handy to experience it. 

  ❄                ❄                ❄                ❄                ❄ 

 That observation particularly resonates with me today, as I’ve just spent the weekend at  Foocamp, an  open space  event run by O’Reilly. It was full of those who are experiencing that vertigo of LLM capabilities, so it felt very much like being in a bubble of those on the bleeding edge. While it’s worth remembering that that’s not a typical view of what’s happening, it’s good to see what those explorers are experiencing. (As I talk about this, I’m applying the  Chatham House rule , if anyone wants attribution, let me know.) 

 A couple of folks talked about doing immersion courses to help teach developers how to do  agentic engineering . The idea is to get people out of their usual work environment, and spend one or two weeks with experienced coaches, learning how to use these tools well. Such things remind me very much of the  Extreme Programming  immersion classes I was involved with at the turn of the century.

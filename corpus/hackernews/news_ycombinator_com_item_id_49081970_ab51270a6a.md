# Why I prefer Opus 5 to Fable 5

> Source: hackernews | Category: news | 2026-07-28T13:12:35Z

Score: 8 | Comments: 1

I&#x27;ve been experimenting with both since the moment each came out. 4 games, 3 websites and 2 content creation engines later - I think I&#x27;m ready to share a bit of my experience and opinion.<p>WHAT I&#x27;VE NOTICED ABOUT FABLE<p>1. Gold in, gold out - meaning I&#x27;ve had the best results by making all my design decisions before giving Fable the kickoff information and prompt. It&#x27;s much more comfortable making decisions by itself - decisions it won&#x27;t tell you about - unless you explicitly tell it what it can and can&#x27;t decide for itself up front.<p>2. It&#x27;s a black box - which is an extention of the point above in a way. I&#x27;ve used the analogy of an agency when I&#x27;ve described it to people, i.e. it&#x27;ll take a brief (what Fable itself describes as &quot;an order&quot;) and go off and run with it, only coming back once it&#x27;s done. I once had a Fable conversation work independently for 9 hours buidling out a client self-service portal for me. All I had to do was type &#x27;Continue&#x27; once it hit its session limits every 5 hours.<p>3. It&#x27;s insanely powerful - so if you&#x27;ve got everything worked out up front by thinking and working through it with an Opus conversation, you can get the most staggering results. It creates its own sub-agents, does its own testing and self-verification, and ships you back a finished product. It blew my mind.<p>BUT I PREFER OPUS 5<p>Why? Because it&#x27;s not a black box.<p>1. It reports back - it seems to pause at natural phase gates and give an update on what it produced and why. There&#x27;s a rhythm to it that keeps you the user involved.<p>2. It&#x27;s just as capable of spinning up sub-agents and doing its own testing and verification.<p>3. It genuinely is a lot cheaper - for example when Fable runs on a task my Claude Max limits burn out in roughly 30 to 45 minutes (Fable is FAST). But then that leaves me twiddling my thumbs. Opus 5 feels more methodical somehow (which might just be because it comes back to the user, resulting in a slower cadence). Or maybe the better way of putting it is that Opus keeps you the developer involved throughout. And it feels like it&#x27;s more interested in giving you exactly what you want. Fable doesn&#x27;t do that - it basically just takes the brief and does its own thing.<p>I simply wanted to share my experience as I&#x27;ve quickly reached the point of familiarity and thought that might be valuable to others who are experimenting with these frontier models.<p>Look forward to your thoughts. Cheers.

# [eff] Voters Reject ALPRs and Surveillance Tech at the Ballot Box

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-18T07:10:53Z

Voters Reject ALPRs and Surveillance Tech at the Ballot Box

Do    automated license plate readers (ALPRs)    have any place in our communities? That question has been a hot topic in    city council meetings   ,    community conversations   , and    news coverage    around the country for months. This past election day, a couple of U.S communities took it further: to the ballot.   

  And overwhelmingly, voters decided that, no, these devices should not be deployed on our streets.    We agree!      

  In Maine, the    coastal community of York voted    to remove the town’s existing ALPR cameras and require that any future proposed installations receive voter approval before proceeding. The local initiative ended up on the ballot after constituents    gathered enough signatures    on a citizens’ petition for a local ordinance. On Election Day, nearly three-quarters of York voters supported the ban.   

  Thousands of miles away, voters in League City, Texas also voted    against allowing the local police department    to continue use of ALPR cameras. That vote was formally approved on November 17, which began a 72-hour window to remove the cameras. Since the election,    the cameras have been “bagged”    or covered to prevent the collection of data.  

  At least one city also decided to expand protections afforded to their residents when it comes to the use of surveillance technology. In Pflugerville, Texas,    Proposition B passed   , now requiring that the City Charter be amended to include “protections governing the collection, use, retention, and oversight of data, facial recognition, and surveillance technologies.” It also requires “a responsible artificial intelligence framework.”   

  While every municipality has different rules for how to get a local ordinance placed on the ballot, banning use and securing the removal of surveillance tools locally is a powerful way of protecting your community against devices and data collection that goes far beyond your town lines.   

  Surveillance is a choice, not a mandate

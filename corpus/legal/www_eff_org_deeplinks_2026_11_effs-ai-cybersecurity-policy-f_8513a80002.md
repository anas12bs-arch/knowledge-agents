# [eff] EFF’s AI Cybersecurity Policy Framework

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-17T01:00:04Z

EFF’s AI Cybersecurity Policy Framework

The news has been filled with stories recently of AI breaching the security of websites. And while it may feel like a new problem because of AI’s involvement, it turns out that there are longstanding existing cybersecurity safety best practices that these companies—some of the largest tech companies in the world—could and should have taken to prevent these breaches. 
 Recent cybersecurity incidents have shown that some of the world’s largest tech companies have failed to take basic security precautions when testing frontier AI systems on internet-connected systems. Unfortunately, these failures resulted in breaches of numerous third-party websites, including an AI research-sharing platform called Hugging Face and the Australian healthcare system’s website. Even worse, AI developers failed to detect most of the incidents until long after they occurred due to inadequate monitoring of the AI systems during testing. Had the companies abided by basic, common sense security protocols, these incidents likely would not have occurred. 
 While the White House and leaders of major AI companies have claimed that no action is necessary, understandably, lawmakers at all levels and of both parties are proposing legislation to create new, additional safety protocols. These incidents may warrant legislative action. However, the good news is that post-incident reports indicate   new   protocols are largely unnecessary. Following   existing   best practices would have been enough to prevent the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident and others like it. In other words, responsible AI developers can take immediate action to protect the public by implementing well-established security precautions as they develop and test their systems.

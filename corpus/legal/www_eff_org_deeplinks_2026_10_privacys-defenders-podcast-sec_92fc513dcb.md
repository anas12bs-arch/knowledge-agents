# [eff] Privacy's Defenders Podcast: The Secret Room

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-04T02:24:31Z

Privacy's Defenders Podcast: The Secret Room

The government’s surveillance powers grew rapidly after Sept. 11, 2001, some in public view and some in secret. EFF suspected but couldn’t prove that the National Security Agency was spying on Americans' communications. The New York Times sat on a story about it for over a year, finally publishing it in December 2005. Then, in January 2006, a retired AT&amp;T technician named    Mark Klein    walked into EFF's office with a manila folder of documents that blew the story wide open.     
  Welcome to the third episode of “Privacy’s Defenders,” a podcast about the people – lawyers, journalists, hackers, and others – who’ve fought to secure your digital liberties since before most people even knew what the internet was.

  In this episode, former EFF Executive Director Cindy Cohn talks with former EFF lawyer    Kevin Bankston   , who was at his desk drafting a lawsuit against AT&amp;T when Klein showed up. Less than two weeks later, EFF sued the phone company on behalf of its own customers in     Hepting v. AT&amp;T    . Cindy speaks again with now-retired Justice Department lawyer Tony Coppolino, who asked for the opportunity to defend the case. He called EFF with a warning after Klein’s documents were filed in court: If they were classified, EFF would be in big trouble. You’ll also hear from Klein himself, and from Carolyn Jewel, a romance novelist and AT&amp;T customer who became the lead plaintiff in    EFF's related case against the NSA   .

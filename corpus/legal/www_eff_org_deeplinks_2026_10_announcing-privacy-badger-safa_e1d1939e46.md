# [eff] Announcing Privacy Badger for Safari on macOS

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-10-19T19:29:25Z

Announcing Privacy Badger for Safari on macOS

Exciting news for Safari users: Privacy Badger is now available on Safari for macOS! You’ll need Safari version 26.5 or higher to use it.  
  Whether you want to protect yourself from data brokers or just don’t want Big Tech profiting off your browsing data, Privacy Badger is here to help. More than 4 million people already use EFF’s free tracker-blocking extension to take a stand against online surveillance. Safari users can now join them and EFF in the fight for a better web.      
   Install Privacy Badger   
   Support the Fight Against Online Tracking   
  Safari support has been one of Privacy Badger’s most    requested    features for years. Thanks to recent improvements in Safari's extension support, we’ve finally made it happen for Safari version 26.5 and higher. Privacy Badger is now available on all major desktop browsers. And before you ask, yes, we will look into supporting  Safari for iOS  once we've ironed out any issues on macOS.  
  Why Install Privacy Badger on Safari?     
   Most websites    contain invisible tracking code that lets companies collect, share, and profit from your personal data. Some of those companies are data brokers that sell your information to anyone willing to pay. Creepy ads are just the tip of the iceberg when it comes to the harms of online tracking: Once your information enters the data broker ecosystem, it’s nearly impossible to know  who buys it  or what they’re doing with it. Privacy Badger blocks online trackers to prevent your browsing data from being used against you.      
  Privacy Badger goes beyond Safari’s    built-in privacy features    to provide additional protection against online tracking. In its default browsing mode, Safari limits the information that trackers can access but still allows them to load. Privacy Badger blocks trackers from loading in the first place, preventing them from tracking you using    techniques    that bypass Safari’s protections.

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-10-21T23:13:45Z

Interoperability vs. Security: Why not both?

An open internet where people can freely build, share, and remix each other's work is a powerful engine of grassroots innovation we’ve all benefited from. In recent decades, however, corporations have taken control of an increasing share of this work, giving them a greater ability to stifle challengers and limit our choices. When regulators address this by mandating that  tech giants play fair by requiring interoperability, the gatekeepers respond with a tired refrain: Playing fair puts users’ security at risk.  
  Unfortunately for these trillion-dollar companies, this     gotcha     isn’t the end of the conversation. Regulators can ensure that meaningful choice    and    security thrive.  
  Lawmakers have been catching up, recognizing that the best way to rein in big tech is to require these    oligopolies    to open up their platforms to independent developers. As we    explained six years ago   , mandating interoperability helps prevent the misuse of people’s data and enables more privacy-respecting alternatives. Tech giants, in contrast, have been arguing that our data is safer when they have exclusive control.  
  Tech Giants Claim Interoperability Means Insecurity  
  The European Union’s    Digital Markets Act    (DMA) put interoperability into law under Article 6(7): “gatekeeper shall allow providers of services and providers of hardware, free of charge, effective interoperability with” a designated    Core Platform Service    they operate. The language describes an unambiguous requirement to remediate vendor lockouts and anti-competitive behavior of the major service operators—it is not optional.  
  Apple    claimed    that enforcing this obligation in mobile app stores would put their customers at risk and do irreparable harm to trust in their brand.

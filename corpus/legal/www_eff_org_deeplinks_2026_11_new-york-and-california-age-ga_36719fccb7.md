# [eff] New York and California Age Gating Rules Go Into Effect in the New Year

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-24T23:49:42Z

New York and California Age Gating Rules Go Into Effect in the New Year

Come January 2027, everyday social media browsing in two of the country's largest states will look noticeably different. In California,    AB 1709    (2026),    AB 1043    (2025), and    SB 976    (2024) are slated to take effect on January 1, 2027. Meanwhile, state    attorney general regulations    for    New York’s Stop Addictive Feeds Exploitation (SAFE) for Kids Act    take effect on January 25, 2027.  
  EFF has opposed these laws across    legislatures   ,    regulatory proceedings   , and    the courts   . The core problem is simple: age-gating mandates trade away everyday user privacy and free expression under the guise of safety, while pushing platforms to gather even more personal data on users.  
  What California’s Rules Say  
  Three interlocking California laws take effect on January 1, 2027, reshaping how platforms handle age checks and design features for young users. First,    SB 976    (2024) requires verifiable    parental consent    before platforms can offer algorithmic feeds or nighttime notifications to anyone under 18. Second,    AB 1709    (2026) goes even further by outright banning "addictive features," including personalized recommendation algorithms, autoplay, and endless scroll, for users under 16, even with parental permission. Third,    the Digital Age Assurance Act (AB 1043, 2025)    mandates age-assurance checks across devices (operating systems) and apps, serving as the enforcement backbone for those restrictions. Together with the    California Attorney General’s proposed rules on SB 976   , which outline age-gating methods, accuracy, circumvention, and parental consent, these laws force platforms into a two-tier design that walls young people off from basic content-discovery tools, puts everyone's privacy at risk to prove their age, and has already drawn First Amendment lawsuits from social media companies against both    SB 976    and    AB 1709   .

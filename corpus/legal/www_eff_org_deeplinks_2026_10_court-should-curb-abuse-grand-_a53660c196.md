# [eff] Court Should Curb Abuse of Grand Jury Subpoenas Targeting Social Media Users

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-10-28T23:11:36Z

Court Should Curb Abuse of Grand Jury Subpoenas Targeting Social Media Users

A federal appeals court should curb the government’s abuse of grand jury subpoenas to unmask social media users who criticize lawless immigration enforcement. 
 That is the message from the ACLU, EFF, and other civil liberties and trade groups who filed  a friend-of-the-court brief  in a sealed case in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia. 
  The case involves  the government’s investigation of an anonymous Reddit user who commented on a post about an immigration officer in Minnesota. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) first issued an administrative subpoena to Reddit to unmask the user. When the user challenged the subpoena in court on free speech and statutory grounds ( as many others have ), DHS withdrew it. But then the government obtained a separate grand jury subpoena—a subpoena issued by a prosecutor to gather evidence to present to a grand jury before any criminal charges are filed—for much of the same information. The user then challenged the grand jury subpoena too, and the case is currently being appealed. 
 The right to speak anonymously is essential. It preserves privacy and shields critics from government retaliation. In the past year, the Department of Justice (DOJ) has issued grand jury subpoenas to social media companies for their users’ personal data. This is frequently done for improper purposes and without really showing that social media users have committed any crime. These subpoenas have the effect of intimidating and chilling critics, as immigration authorities have engaged in  invasive ,  unconstitutional , and  sometimes violent conduct . 
 As the amicus brief makes clear, courts should not defer to the government’s claims regarding the legitimacy of using grand jury subpoenas to unmask internet users. Instead, the subpoenas must receive proper First Amendment scrutiny. Like many other types of legal processes, grand jury subpoenas are issued by federal officials without prior court approval.

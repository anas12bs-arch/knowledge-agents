# [eff] The Justice Department Wants To Give Patent Trolls More Power

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-18T00:07:17Z

The Justice Department Wants To Give Patent Trolls More Power

Software patents    are mostly vague, hard to define, and widely abused. Most tech patent lawsuits aren’t filed by companies that make real products. They’re filed by patent trolls, companies that acquire patents to extract payments through litigation and legal threats.   
  A patent on one feature of a smartphone shouldn’t give the patent owner the power to threaten the existence of the entire product line. But that is exactly the leverage patent trolls want—and the current administration is helping them to get it.   
  In two recent patent cases,    Radian Memory Systems v. Samsung    and    Collision Communications v. Samsung,    the Justice Department and the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office have asked courts to make it    easier    for patent owners to  get  injunctions. These are judicial orders that can force companies to stop selling products or remove features from them, rather than simply pay monetary damages.   
  The threat of an injunction can pressure a company to pay far more than what a patent is actually worth. In 2006, NTP Inc., a    patent holding company    with no products of its own, was able to pressure BlackBerry manufacturer Research in Motion Ltd. to pay a $612 million settlement when it was on the verge of an injunction.   
  Protection Against Patent Trolls  
  In May 2006, the Supreme Court rejected the practice of granting permanent injunctions almost automatically after a finding of patent infringement. In     eBay v. MercExchange    , the high court held that patent owners must satisfy the same tough requirements that plaintiffs in non-patent cases must meet to get a permanent injunction. After eBay, patent owners had to show “irreparable harm,” that they can’t be adequately compensated by money, that the “balance of hardships” favors the plaintiff, and, critically, that an injunction would not harm the “public interest.”

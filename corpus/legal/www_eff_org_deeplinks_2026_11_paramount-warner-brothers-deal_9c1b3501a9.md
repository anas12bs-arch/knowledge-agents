The Paramount-Warner Brothers Deal Could Usher in Another Age of Lost Media

There are all sorts of reasons to be against the merger of Paramount and Warner Brothers-Discovery. There's the obvious problem of increasing concentration in the already pretty-concentrated film and TV industry. People have problems with Paramount's owner Skydance interfering  with its    news    coverage and    late night comedy   . But one of the biggest is the way we are losing choice and access to information and culture.      
  In the context of the digital world, there are two major problems with these mega mergers. One, as we've said in the past, is that it    further degrades    the already awful state of mainstream streaming services. In short, by increasing the amount of legacy content under their control, major studios don't have to invest in either new content or well-engineered services to get users. Plus, they can pool the data they have on users together in an increasing assault on consumer privacy. A monthly subscription and more information about your habits and interests? Win-win for them.      
  Another problem is the disappearing of content. In the digital era, there should be no more lost media. Making and storing copies of media is easier and cheaper than it ever has been. The idea of the persistence of content online has become the adage "the internet never forgets."      
  However, since more and more media is locked behind subscription services under the control of fewer and fewer companies—and as physical copies, or even just digital copies that are actually    owned    by consumers also vanish—access to our common culture is at risk. Incentives to take information and media offline should raise huge red flags. Unfortunately, they are multiplying.

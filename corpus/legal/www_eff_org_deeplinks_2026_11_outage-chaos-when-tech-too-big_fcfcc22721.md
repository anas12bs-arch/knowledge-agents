# [eff] Outage Chaos: When Tech is too Big to Fail

> Source: legal | Category: legal | 2026-11-18T23:00:52Z

Outage Chaos: When Tech is too Big to Fail

Outages happen, but we deserve a resilient internet where no one outage at one company can put people at risk and disrupt the global economy. We can only get there by addressing an over-reliance on a few cloud monopolists through effective antitrust laws and interoperability requirements.  
  Service outages, like we’ve seen recently at    Microsoft   ,    Apple   *,    banks   ,    courts   ,    airlines   , and elsewhere, can be more than an annoying hurdle. Losing a vital service due to an error can cause the same impacts as a government-ordered internet shutdown—people losing access to vital information and lines of communication.  As usual, the greatest harms are faced by people on the margins globally.  
  There’s not much hope in companies out-nerding these issues either. Technical errors will always exist, whether it’s    a bug   , negligence from an administrator, or a malicious attack. The problem is an over-reliance on any one company or system when these inevitable outages occur. It’s a matter of public safety and economic stability that policymakers mitigate the impact of these disruptions before they occur.   
  In order to do so, we must ensure antitrust prevents providers of key technical systems from becoming too big to fail.  
  The harms inflicted by tech monopolies go beyond monetary costs to the end user. In fact, monopolists can often offer lower prices, or even offer free services, thanks to their greater scale and incentive to shut out competitors    using free-flowing investment capital   . The problem is this puts a few tech companies in a position of incredible influence. The true cost is a loss of control when our private data, access to knowledge, and free expression all become mediated by tools owned by a handful of billionaires.  
  Losing diversity in our digital infrastructure creates a less stable and less resilient internet for everyone.
